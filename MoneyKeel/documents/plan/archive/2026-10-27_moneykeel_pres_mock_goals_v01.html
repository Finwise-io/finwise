The household, on 26 October 2026. Born 1975, working; $6,000/month of wages before tax, at the 20% rate typed: $4,800 of take-home. A budget of $1,500 bills and fixed, $200 non-monthly and $800 everyday: $2,500/month. A Visa card, $9,000 at 24%, minimum $200, payment set at $300; a car loan, $14,000 at 6.9%, $250. One goal: an emergency fund, $4,000 of $10,000 by October 2027, nothing funded this month. Extra typed: $100.
The figures, by hand. Free cash: $4,800 − $2,500 − ($300 + $250) = $1,750. Sinking fund: $200 × 12 = $2,400/year. The goal: $10,000 − $4,000 = $6,000, ÷ 12 months = $500/month; 4,000 ÷ 10,000 = 40.00%. Surplus this month: $4,800 in, nothing logged out (on the actual side nothing is assumed paid, DEC-1923). Debt-to-income: the minimums, $200 + $250 = $450, ÷ $6,000 = 7.50%. The plan pays $300 + $250 + $100 = $650 a month, the Visa first (the highest rate): the Visa clears in month 31 (May 2029), the car loan in month 44 (Jun 2030), $5,409.45 of interest. With no extra, $550 a month: month 56 (Jun 2031), $7,646.60. The extra saves $7,646.60 − $5,409.45 = $2,237.15, printed $2,237, and 56 − 44 = 12 months. (On the minimums alone, $450 a month, it would be month 84 and $14,786.87 — the figure the old words implied.)
