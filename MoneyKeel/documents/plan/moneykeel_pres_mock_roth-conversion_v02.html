WHAT IT BUYS LATER
Converting moves $20,000 out of the money the law makes you take out from 75, into money that comes out tax-free.
Pre-tax (IRA, 401(k))$415,000 → $395,000

How: the $20,000 would grow at 7.62%/year — the blended benchmark — for 15 years, to $60,151. ÷ 24.6, the IRS factor at 75 = $2,445/year less you must take out, and less income to pay tax on then.
Roth money is never required out, and comes out tax-free.
WILL MY MONEY LAST?
Today, the odds that your $523,000 lasts to age 90 are 35.3%.
Converting $20,000 this year changes your nest egg at 65 to about $809,588 (from $813,149) and the odds to 36.2%.
This decision is about taxes, not the odds.
How: the nest egg at 65 is the Retirement cockpit’s — today’s money grown at the plan’s return, with what you add each year; converted, it starts $3,750 smaller. The odds count 1,000 simulated futures, run twice — as you are, and with $20,000 moved from pre-tax to Roth and the $3,750 tax paid from your cash: more of the money then comes out tax-free.

The household, on 27 October 2026 (v01’s). Born 1966 (60 this year), single; $70,000 of wages, $10,000 of it into a 401(k); Checking $20,000, a Fidelity IRA $415,000, a Roth IRA $88,000; spending $4,500/month; retire at 65, plan to 90 (the Plan’s defaults). $20,000 typed to convert.
The figures, by hand. The tax: the year is taxed on $70,000 − $10,000 = $60,000; federal tax on $60,000 − $16,100 = $43,900 → $1,240 + 12% × $31,500 = $5,020; on $80,000: $63,900 → $1,240 + $4,560 + 22% × $13,500 = $8,770; the conversion costs $8,770 − $5,020 = $3,750, 18.75% of $20,000. What it buys: pre-tax $415,000 − $20,000 = $395,000; Roth $88,000 + $20,000 = $108,000. The return: ($415,000 × 7.90% + $88,000 × 7.90% + $20,000 × 0.50%) ÷ $523,000 = 7.617%. In 15 years (to 75, born 1966): $415,000 × 1.07617¹⁵ = $1,248,137 ÷ 24.6 = $50,737; $395,000 × 1.07617¹⁵ = $1,187,986 ÷ 24.6 = $48,292; $50,737 − $48,292 = $2,445, which is also $20,000 × 1.07617¹⁵ = $60,151 ÷ 24.6. The odds: the nest egg the odds run on is $20,000 + $415,000 + $88,000 = $523,000; after the tax, $523,000 − $3,750 = $519,250 (Checking $16,250, IRA $395,000, Roth $108,000). 1,000 simulated futures each time: 35.3% as you are, 36.2% converted. The nest egg at 65, the cockpit’s own figure: $523,000 grown 5 years at 7.617% with $10,000 added at the end of each year = $813,149 (the cockpit’s $813K); converted, $519,250 grown at 7.668% — less cash in the mix: ($395,000 × 7.90% + $108,000 × 7.90% + $16,250 × 0.50%) ÷ $519,250 — with the same $10,000 a year = $809,588; $3,562 less.
