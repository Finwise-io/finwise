Are the retirement figures right? The arithmetic follows from what the calculation is given. Today’s engine, run on this household, gives 87.2% odds, about $4,124,000 at 65 and about $12,711,000 at 92; Build 53 printed $4.3M and $11.4M because the engine changed after it. The figures overstate in four ways:
  1. They are future dollars. At 2.5% inflation, $4,124,000 at 65 is about $1,966,000 in today’s money, and $12,711,000 at 92 is about $3,111,000. The sentence does not say which.
  2. They are the middle of 1,000 futures. Half end lower, and in the worst 10 of every 100 the money is gone by 92.
  3. Withdrawals are not taxed. The savings sit in a taxable brokerage, and the calculation charges no tax when they are drawn.
  4. Only the $4,500 a month is charged. That is $113,269 a year at 65, rising 2.5% a year; no Social Security was entered. The $1,200 a month of saving stays flat for 30 years, and the return is 7.77% a year. The mortgage is paid from pay and, by my arithmetic, ends around 2054, before retirement.
My recommendation: show the figures in today’s money, and charge tax on withdrawals from taxable accounts. Say yes, and both go on the master as findings before any code changes.
