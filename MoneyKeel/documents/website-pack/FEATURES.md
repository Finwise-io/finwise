# MoneyKeel: what it is and what it does

## In one line

**Your whole money picture in one calm place, and every number shows how it was made.**

## The short version

MoneyKeel is a calm personal-finance app for iPhone. It keeps a household's whole picture in one place: net worth, cash flow, investment performance and a retirement plan. Every number shows how it was made. MoneyKeel never guesses, and when it doesn't know something, it says so plainly instead of showing $0.

## Who it is for

- Adults of about 36 to 65 who are steering toward retirement, and people already retired (65 and over).
- Households with savings, retirement accounts, a home, and perhaps a rental property or a pension.
- People who want clarity and trust more than excitement. They want to know where they stand and whether their money will last, in plain words.

## The name

A keel is the spine along the bottom of a boat. It keeps the boat steady and on course in rough water. MoneyKeel does the same for a household's money: it is steady and quiet, and it keeps you pointed where you mean to go.

## What it does

### Home: the one-glance summary
- Your investments and how they did this month, side by side with the market over the same days. Both figures count dividends and interest.
- Your net worth in one line, and whether it went up or down.
- What needs you: the top three things worth your attention, each with a dollar amount. On a quiet day it simply says nothing needs you.
- Will my money last: your retirement odds in one line. If you are retired, Home leads with your monthly paycheck.

### Net worth: everything you own, less everything you owe
- One figure, with the two totals that make it shown right above it: "Own $813,152 − Owe $418,000".
- View it by category (cash, investments, property) or by bank and broker. Covers cash, stocks and funds, bonds and CDs, real estate, other investments such as crypto, gold and annuities, and debts such as mortgages, cards and loans.
- Every account says where its number comes from: linked, or entered by hand. The costliest debt is marked "pay first".
- Emergency fund: how many months of essential expenses your cash would cover, with the sum written out ($8,838 cash ÷ $4,500 a month = 2.0 months).
- Tap the change to see exactly how net worth moved: money in, money out, dividends, interest, market change and debt paid down.
- Add an account three ways: link it (read-only), add it by hand, or import the file your brokerage exports. If something is missing, a banner names each gap and shows how to fix it.

### Performance: how your investments really did
- Your return for five periods: since you started, the last month, this year, one year and two years.
- Measured the way professionals measure it ("time-weighted"), so money you add is never counted as a gain. Dividends and interest are included. A period longer than a year is shown as a yearly rate, with the total beside it.
- Wealth generated: what your money earned by itself (dividends, interest and change in value), kept apart from what you put in.
- A step-by-step walk, such as "How $38,000 became $52,704", and what each category earned. When a return can't be measured, the screen shows a dash and says why, so a guess is never presented as a figure.

### Account, holding and bond pages
- Each account has its own page with the same five periods, its own return, every holding with its value, return and income, and a full list of activity. Records from your broker stay as the broker sent them. Anything you add is marked as yours and stays editable.
- A single holding shows its live price and price chart, what you paid, how it did against the market over the same dates, and your gain if you sold today, split into long-term and short-term.
- Bonds and CDs show face value, coupon rate, yield to maturity and maturity date. The bond page also estimates what the bond might be worth if interest rates rose or fell by one point.
- A holding with no live price says so and shows what you paid. Nothing is invented.

### Cash flow: money in and money out
- Each month, your plan side by side with what has actually happened so far: take-home pay in, bills and spending out, and the surplus left over. Step back to any earlier month.
- Four tabs: This month, Income, Expenses and Debts. Set a budget, and add expenses by hand or from a photo of a receipt. A "big-ticket radar" picks out card payments and any bill well above its usual size.
- Income from salary, rental property, stock grants and more, for one or two earners. Take-home pay is worked out after tax and 401(k).
- Debts: what you owe, the monthly payments, which debt to pay first, and how much you have paid down. Your mortgage rate sits beside what a Treasury bond pays, with the difference in dollars, so you can weigh paying it down faster. The app shows the numbers and leaves the choice to you.

### Plan: will my money last?
- Your odds, found by running your own numbers through 1,000 possible market futures and shown plainly: "your money lasts in about 70 out of 100 simulated futures".
- Your retirement paycheck: Social Security plus pension plus what you can draw from savings, a month at a time.
- Decisions you can make today, each worked out with your numbers: when to claim Social Security, whether converting to a Roth is worth it, required withdrawals that are due, whether you can spend more, which account to draw from first, and when you can retire.
- What-ifs: see what saving more each month would do, or test a surprise expense against your cash. Trying something changes nothing until you choose it. You can also look back ("What if I'd moved money 12 months ago?") using real past prices, not a prediction.
- Goals (a car, college, a wedding) and a Prepare list (insurance, estate, tax organiser, a job-loss check).

### Privacy and control
- Linked accounts are read-only. You sign in on your broker's own page, and MoneyKeel never sees or stores your password. The app has no way to move money or make trades.
- If you unlink an account, you choose whether to keep its numbers as a hand-entered account or delete them.
- Hide every balance with one tap, and lock the app with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode.

## What makes it different

- **Every figure shows its working.** A total always adds up from the rows beneath it, every change can be traced line by line, and an estimate is labelled as an estimate.
- **Unknown is never shown as $0.** If something hasn't been entered or can't be measured, the screen says "not entered" or shows a dash, and says why. It never shows a zero that looks like an answer.
- **Returns measured the way professionals do.** Time-weighted returns that include dividends and interest, so adding money never looks like growth and income is never left out.
- **Linked accounts are read-only.** MoneyKeel can look but never touch. It has no trading or transfer commands at all.
- **Plain words, one word per idea.** The same thing is called the same name on every screen, written for readers aged 55 to 70. Each screen says what a number means, not only what it is.
- **Straight talk about your own money.** Tax, risk and cost are stated plainly, and nothing is made to look better than it is. MoneyKeel lays out the numbers and you decide. No ads, and your information is never sold.
