Income — every screen, one document

The whole income surface: the picker, each source it opens, the new tax question, and the summary that reads it all back. The rules underneath

1 · One question decides what gets asked. Ten sources; only the ones picked ever appear, and the seven not picked stay one tap away rather than hidden.
2 · One section per thing, opening on its own. A screen is never longer than the thing being filled in, whether that is one earner or two.
2b · One component, two frames. Onboarding pushes the same screen with a progress bar and Continue; Cash flow → Income opens it with a back arrow and Save. Two copies of the same questions is how they come to be asked two different ways.
3 · Each earner has their own pay pattern — frequency and steady-or-varies — and their month table lives inside their own section.
4 · Three figures that must reconcile. Gross, the 401(k), and the take-home. One figure alone can never be checked; three catch each other, within $5 a month.
5 · Nothing is grossed up, and nothing is subtracted that was not entered. The take-home someone enters IS the take-home; with no 401(k) given, none is deducted.
6 · The tax bracket is read from gross, annualised — average month × 12, never the total of a fourteen-month window, and never from take-home, which also carries medical cover and anything else bought through work.
7 · A lump counts in its own month. A December bonus makes December different; spreading it makes all twelve wrong to make one look tidy.
8 · Two totals, never one. Take-home is not what it is taxed on, and each says which it is. Interest and dividends are the clearest case: taxed, and never spendable unless someone says they take them as cash. [Founder 2026-08-27]
8b · Never ask for what can be calculated. The app holds the accounts, so it shows what they pay and lets the person correct it. Asking invites a second, different answer to the same question with nothing to reconcile them.
8e · The card adds up, and every figure says whose it is. Gross − tax − 401(k)s = take-home, on the card itself, in every state. Entered beats calculated, per person, never blended; a mixed household shows no single tax row, because one number cannot explain a figure that is half theirs and half ours. Assumptions are stated where they act: “assumed filing jointly — both of you earn”. [Founder 2026-09-02]
8d · Name the bracket, and say what the estimate assumed. “Federal tax bracket” and “estimated federal tax — assumes you take the standard deduction”. Never “effective tax rate”: three products compute it three ways, so it is a number a person cannot check. One rate, typed once, used for every tax figure in the app. [Founder 2026-08-28]
\n8c · A figure is corrected where it is shown. The tax rate could only be changed on a screen buried in the guided questionnaire; it belongs beside the number it moves. And an estimate says what it leaves out in MONEY, beside itself — never only as a percentage. [Founder 2026-08-28]
9 · Nothing is guessed. Every estimate is prefixed ≈ and says what it cannot see: no state tax, no insurance, one income unless a spouse’s is given.
