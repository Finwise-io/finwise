Cash flow — planned and actual, kept apart

Two tabs, four states. Every money row carries a Planned or Actual tag, and each has exactly one helper behind it. Planned = take-home − each debt − planned expenses. Actual = take-home − only the debts confirmed paid − what has actually been logged.

What this fixes. Today one card says Out (bills + debt) $8,321 and four rows later “Spent $45 of $0 planned”. Those are two different definitions of planned — the dated grid, and the itemised categories — differing by the whole amount. The $45 is a third thing again: actual. Three meanings, one card, none labelled.

The rules underneath

1 · Every money row is Planned or Actual, never both. One helper behind each, so two rows can never disagree.
2 · Debts are listed one by one, not lumped into “bills + debt”. In Actual only a debt confirmed paid counts; the rest show their due date at $0.
2b · Recurring debt sits in its own section; the radar only warns about the unusual. The split is one the data already makes — installment debt (mortgage, car, student loan) is the same every month and is never a radar item; revolving debt (credit card, HELOC) and a due-in-full lump vary, so a payment well above its usual IS one. Nothing new to capture.
3 · Planned expenses come from one place — the spending plan — and every “of $X planned” on any screen reads that same number.
4 · Nothing is guessed. With no plan set, the app says “not set” and offers the way to set it. It never prints $0 as if it were an answer.
5 · The numbers add up on the page. 4,818 − 2,450 − 480 − 195 − 5,196 = −3,503, and 4,818 − 2,450 − 45 = +2,323.
