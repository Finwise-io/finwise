Add income — ask for all three, derive the rest

Base pay is one field today with a Gross / Take-home toggle, and the 401(k) is asked on another screen entirely. This asks for gross, what goes into the 401(k) and what lands in the bank in one place — so nothing has to be guessed.

What it buys. With all three, tax stops being estimated and becomes derived: gross − 401(k) − what lands in the bank = everything else taken out. That covers federal, state, insurance and anything else on the payslip, without asking about any of them. Today the app grosses a typed take-home figure back up and taxes it down again — which double-subtracts the 401(k) and lands $1,467 a month away from what the person typed.

The rules underneath

1 · Gross is the only required figure. The other two are optional, and the screen says what each one buys.
2 · Nothing is grossed up, ever. If they say what lands in the bank, that is what every screen shows. The old road — gross a typed take-home figure back up, then tax it down — removed the 401(k) twice and landed $1,467 a month out.
3 · Tax is derived, not estimated, when all three are given. gross − 401(k) − what lands = everything else taken out. That covers federal, state, insurance and anything else, without asking about any of them.
4 · Every estimate says it is one. With only gross, the take-home line is prefixed ≈ and says what it does not know.
5 · The three must reconcile. If they cannot, the screen says which is likely wrong and why, and Save waits.
6 · The 401(k) is asked here, once. It is asked on a different screen today, which is how it came to be subtracted twice.
