Your base pay — stable or variable, and all three figures

Base pay is one field today with a Gross / Take-home toggle, and the 401(k) is asked on another screen entirely. This asks for gross, what goes into the 401(k) and what lands in the bank in one place — so nothing has to be guessed. One question first: is your pay the same every month? Stable asks each figure once; variable pre-fills twelve boxes from what was entered, ready to be corrected where a month differs.

What it buys. With all three, tax stops being estimated and becomes derived: gross − 401(k) − what lands in the bank = everything else taken out. That covers federal, state, insurance and anything else on the payslip, without asking about any of them. Today the app grosses a typed take-home figure back up and taxes it down again — which double-subtracts the 401(k) and lands $1,467 a month away from what the person typed.

Founder ruling, 2026-08-27: never subtract a 401(k) nobody has entered. The estimate deducts tax only, and says so.

The rules underneath

1 · Gross is the only required figure. The other two are optional, and the screen says what each one buys.
1b · One question decides the shape: is your pay the same every month? The same → each figure asked once. It varies → twelve boxes for gross and for what lands, two months per row with the year on each, running from LAST MONTH to twelve ahead. Every box starts from the typical-month figure, so nobody types fourteen numbers. The 401(k) stays one figure either way. [Founder 2026-08-27]
1c · Never subtract what was not entered. With no 401(k) given, the estimate deducts tax only and says so. [Founder 2026-08-27]
2 · Nothing is grossed up, ever. If they say what lands in the bank, that is what every screen shows. The old road — gross a typed take-home figure back up, then tax it down — removed the 401(k) twice and landed $1,467 a month out.
3 · The tax bracket is read from GROSS, annualised — never from take-home. Average the months given and multiply by twelve: 14 months totalling $103,000 is an average of $7,357, so $88,286 a year, not $103,000. Summing the window instead would tax fourteen months of pay as if it were twelve — $3,237 too much. And take-home can never be used for this: it also carries medical cover and anything else bought through work, so it understates the bracket. [Founder 2026-08-27]
3b · Tax is derived, not estimated, when all three are given. gross − 401(k) − what lands = everything else taken out. That covers federal, state, insurance and anything else, without asking about any of them.
4 · Every estimate says it is one. With only gross, the take-home line is prefixed ≈ and says what it does not know.
5 · The three must reconcile, within $5. That absorbs someone rounding their own figures and still catches a wrong frequency. If they cannot, the screen says which is likely wrong and why, and Save waits. On variable pay every month is checked on its own, not only the year. [Founder 2026-08-27]
5b · Deductions over 60% of gross warn, but never block. It usually means a figure was entered at the wrong frequency — but some people really do have deductions that high, and we do not tell them their own payslip is impossible. [Founder 2026-08-27]
6 · The 401(k) is asked here, once. It is asked on a different screen today, which is how it came to be subtracted twice.
