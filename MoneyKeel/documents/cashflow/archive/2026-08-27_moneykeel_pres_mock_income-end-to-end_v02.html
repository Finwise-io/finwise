Income — every screen, one document

The whole income surface: the picker, each source it opens, the new tax question, and the summary that reads it all back. Numbers reconcile across every screen — $167,400 gross a year, $9,500 a month landing — and the $250 of dividends that is taxed but never reaches the bank. Supersedes every earlier income mock; those are in archive/.

Three patterns run through all of it. One question decides what gets asked, so nobody sees a screen they do not need. Each answer sits in a section that opens on its own, so a screen is never longer than the thing you are filling in. And every figure says what it is worth before you leave it, so nothing is entered blind.

The rules underneath

1 · One question decides what gets asked. Ten sources; only the ones picked ever appear, and the seven not picked stay one tap away rather than hidden.
2 · One section per thing, opening on its own. A screen is never longer than the thing being filled in, whether that is one earner or two.
3 · Each earner has their own pay pattern — frequency and steady-or-varies — and their month table lives inside their own section.
4 · Three figures that must reconcile. Gross, the 401(k), and what lands. One figure alone can never be checked; three catch each other, within $5 a month.
5 · Nothing is grossed up, and nothing is subtracted that was not entered. What someone says lands IS what lands; with no 401(k) given, none is deducted.
6 · The tax bracket is read from gross, annualised — average month × 12, never the total of a fourteen-month window, and never from take-home, which also carries medical cover and anything else bought through work.
7 · A lump lands in its own month. A December bonus makes December different; spreading it makes all twelve wrong to make one look tidy.
8 · Two totals, never one. What lands is not what it is taxed on, and each says which it is. Interest and dividends are the clearest case: taxed, and never spendable unless someone says they take them as cash. [Founder 2026-08-27]
8b · Never ask for what can be worked out. The app holds the accounts, so it shows what they pay and lets the person correct it. Asking invites a second, different answer to the same question with nothing to reconcile them.
9 · Nothing is guessed. Every estimate is prefixed ≈ and says what it cannot see: no state tax, no insurance, one income unless a partner’s is given.
