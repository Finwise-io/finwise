The order I would build it in

Two rules decide the order: a change that moves no number goes in alone and first, and a change that moves a number people will notice goes in where it can be seen and explained.

  1. Months keyed by date. Alone, first, on its own. Reaches 39 functions and moves no arithmetic — the easiest thing in the world to verify by itself, and very hard to verify tangled with new maths. Everything else needs it.
  2. Delete the gross-up branch and salaryMode. Dead code once gross is always captured. Zero functions affected.
  3. Collapse the three employer-match copies to one. No number moves today.
  4. Amend base-pay.ts to two earners and add the household orchestrator. Nothing is wired yet, so this costs a morning now and the wiring too if it waits.
  5. The filing-status question, and over-65 for a couple. Data plus a screen. Five surfaces get the right answer for the first time; a couple sees $328–$866 a month more.
  6. The bracket from both incomes. Needs 4 and 5 done first.
  7. Dividends out of take-home. Last of the number-movers, because it is the only one that makes a figure SMALLER — every surplus drops by the dividend amount. People notice a reduction, so it should land on its own with the reason on the screen.
  8. Dividends from the holdings. Reaches nothing today; safe whenever.
