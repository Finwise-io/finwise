The pattern worth keeping

Four of the defects found in these two days were mine, and every one was caught by a gate rather than by reading: the header guard caught my printed view leaking back as an author's note; the widened sweep exposed 11 duplicate names where I had counted 5; a law you already had caught a wrong waterfall; and opening a screen showed a finding I had reported from the file was simply wrong.

Your own note on the screen-map guard — each caught a real defect within minutes of being written, after the same surfaces had been read carefully by hand — held again, three times over. The practical consequence: a law that must hold for every input is worth more than an example. Of the guards still to build, D3 — the same metric rendered on two screens and compared — is that kind.
