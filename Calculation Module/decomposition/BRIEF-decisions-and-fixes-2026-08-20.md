1. **The cut rate is high — 14 metrics cut, 3 deferred — and that is deliberate.** The questions came

## A · Standing rules that came out of this session

These are not per-metric decisions. They apply everywhere and should be enforced as lint rules,
copy rules or shared modules rather than remembered.

| # | Rule | Where it came from |
|---|---|---|
| **R1** | **Never use the word "safe."** Nobody can guarantee anything. This kills "safe to spend" *and* "safe withdrawal rate." | Q33 |
| **R2** | **An unknown input produces no number and a reason — never a default.** No assumed gain percentage, no assumed tax rate, no guessed lot date, no invented benefit. | Q4, Q12, Q43, Q44 |
| **R3** | **Show the fact, not the verdict.** Ratios and figures are displayed without grading. The person decides what is too much. | Q21, Q24, Q46 |
| **R4** | **A tax figure requires a YEAR.** Net worth has no year, so it shows no tax. The retirement plan has one, so it is the only place tax appears. | Q12, Q51 |
| **R5** | **Class and liquidity are separate fields.** Neither derives from the other. A money-market fund is a fund by class and liquid by tier. | Q7 |
| **R6** | **Precision and display are separate layers.** Full-precision decimal throughout; round ONCE at the edge. Round last, never round twice. | Q56 |
| **R7** | **Source always, date only when stale.** A visible date is a signal that something needs attention. | Q55 |
| **R8** | **One mechanism per shape.** Loan payments, stepped part-time income and health premiums are all *amount + start year + end year + growth rate*. Do not build a third variant. | Q45, Q47 |

## B · Fixes that change existing numbers

These correct figures the app shows today. **They are the priority**, and several move the odds by
more than the sampling noise does.

### B1 — Flat pensions are being inflated (Q5)

The run raises **all** guaranteed income with inflation. Social Security does inflate; most private
pensions are flat. Over 30 years at 2.5% this roughly **doubles** a flat pension.

**Fix:** add a fixed-versus-inflating flag per income stream at capture. Default Social Security to
inflating, pensions and annuities to flat. Visible and editable next to the amount. The default is
safe because its error direction is conservative — today's behaviour is not.

*This is the largest single arithmetic error in the plan engine.*

### B2 — Social Security factors assume full retirement age 67 (Q40)

`SS_CLAIM_FACTOR` hard-codes 0.70 / 1.00 / 1.24 anchored at 67. People born 1956–1959 have full
retirement ages between 66y4m and 66y10m — roughly the **oldest third** of a 55–70 audience, and the
ones closest to claiming.

**Fix, two halves:**
- Add the full-retirement-age table by birth year (twelve rows) and derive the factor from it.
- **The input instruction is also wrong.** The app asks for "the age-67 monthly figure"; a statement
  shows the benefit at *their* FRA. Ask which age the figure is for, or ask for the FRA amount and
  derive the rest. Otherwise a wrong input gets compounded by the factor table.

Confirm the current FRA schedule against SSA guidance before implementing.

### B3 — The legacy target only counts once retired (Q41 rider, work list 19)

A legacy target is subtracted from the pot **only** when the person is already retired. On a test
plan the odds were identical with and without a $200,000 legacy while still working, then dropped
26% → 8% on the retirement date.

**Fix:** subtract it in both cases. One line in `WILL_IT_LAST_INPUTS`. The years when the plan could
still be changed are currently the years it says nothing.

(The estate *screen* is phase 2 and out of scope. This fix is not.)

### B4 — RMD start age is a literal, in two places (Q39)

**Fix:** adopt the birth-year rule, exempt Roth 401(k)s, and remove the duplicate. The real defect is
that a rule which has changed by legislation more than once lives as a literal in two files. One
dated, sourced table, one place. Confirm thresholds against IRS guidance.

### B5 — Withdrawals and RMDs are counted as guaranteed income (Q38)

Counting them lets the pot fund itself: the odds rise the more the person spends.

**Fix:** two separate fields, two roles.
- **Paycheck display** — every line, listed separately: Social Security, pension, portfolio
  withdrawal, RMD. This is what the person can count on next month.
- **Plan's guaranteed-income input** — outside money only. The withdrawal line is what the simulation
  *computes*.
- Nothing is typed into both.
- For "how much can I take," use a **slider** — monthly draw against the age the money lasts to.
  Never a single figure, and per R1 never the word "safe."

### B6 — The odds figure needs calibration (Q46)

The plan screen stays simple: **the odds plus an explanation of what it means.** None of the
following changes anything a user sees.

- Raise the path count above 400 — at 400 draws an 85% result carries roughly ±3.5 points, so two
  identical plans can read 82% and 88%.
- Round the displayed figure to the nearest 5%.
- **Drop the "best-to-worst range" from the screen.** It is two extra runs at different return
  assumptions — a sensitivity test, not a distribution band. Removing it is simpler than relabelling
  it.
- Fix B1, B2 and B3 first. Each moves the number by more than the sampling noise does; a precise
  figure on biased inputs is precisely wrong.

### B7 — Money-market classification (Q2)

The Vanguard position was **clearly labelled money market and still counted as cash** — the broker-tag
path overriding the name.

**Fix:** name-first classification on **every** road, broker tag as fallback only. One classifier
(work list item 3). Classify as a fund only on evidence — cash-equivalent flag, ticker list, name
match, or the person's own answer, which outranks all of them and must survive the next sync.
**An unlabelled broker balance is cash.**

A false negative is cheap (the money is still in net worth, dividends still land as income). A false
positive moves money out of the emergency-reserve figure. When a holding changes class between syncs,
keep the previous class and flag it rather than moving the money quietly.

### B8 — Decimal arithmetic (Q56)

Do this **before** the ledger fixes, so the reconciliation assertions are exact from day one.

Binary floating point means `holdings + cash = the broker's stated total` can never be exact, so a
tolerance has to be set — and then a small mismatch can never be told apart from a real bug. **That is
how the money-market double-count hid.**

Decimal type end to end, including database columns and JSON serialisation. Then round once at
display per R6.

## D · Build and change

### D1 — Liquidity, with a definition (Q7, Q8)

**"Liquid means reachable without losing too much value."** Stated in the info dot, in those words.

| Tier | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Liquid** | Checking, savings, money market, sweep, CDs and bonds maturing within ~3 weeks |
| **Sellable** | Stocks, funds, longer bonds — days, at whatever the market says that morning |
| **Locked** | CDs and bonds before maturity; retirement accounts before the penalty age |
| **Not spendable** | House, car |

Liquid net worth counts the **first tier only** — it will be much smaller than other apps show, and
that is the point. Retirement accounts tagged by **age**, not type. Pre-penalty money excluded
entirely, shown as locked with the age it unlocks. Defaults by class, editable, never asked at
add-time. Per R5, liquidity is its own field.

### D2 — Hand-entered revaluations (Q14)

Mark **all** hand-entered revaluations, not just property, with a mark distinct from deposits,
carrying the previous entry date. Newly connected accounts get the same treatment — a $200,000 step
on link day is not growth.

For the change itself:
- **Market-priced hand entries** (stocks, funds, private holdings, crypto) → **spread evenly across
  the gap.** The movement was real; evenly is the least-wrong distribution absent daily prices. This
  also stops a data-entry date corrupting period returns.
- **House, car, collectibles** → **book on the day**, marked as a revaluation. A re-estimate is not
  market movement, and spreading invents a smooth history there is no evidence for.

Optional per-entry toggle on the edit screen, defaulted by that rule. Any period return whose window
contains a booked revaluation is flagged.

### D3 — Window boundaries (Q3, Q30)

**Both boundary days inside**, app-wide, portfolio and spending alike. Two consequences that must
ship with it or it double-counts:

- Beginning value is the close of the day **before** the start — 30 June for a July window.
- Dietz weights run over the inclusive day count: a start-day flow carries full weight, a last-day
  flow essentially none.

Consecutive windows then tile cleanly. **No smoothing anywhere:** cash flow books non-monthly bills on
the day they occur, the TTM ratio contains each annual bill once by construction, the plan takes
annual amounts.

### D4 — Return method and thresholds (Q1, Q16)

The Performance screen keeps Ending Value, Wealth Generated and Return. **The Return figure stays
money-weighted** — wealth generated is already money-weighted in spirit, so a time-weighted figure
beside it would disagree irreconcilably. Time-weighted is computed only if an index comparison is
built.

Thresholds, all in one named module:
- **1%** — a fallback for when flow **dates** are missing only. With dates, Modified Dietz is always
  available and always better.
- **Timing-sensitivity band replaces the 10% gate.** Compute the rate with all flows at period start
  and again at period end; refuse when the band exceeds ~20% of the figure or ~1 percentage point,
  whichever is tighter, and show the dollar walk instead. Large flows break a rate because Dietz's
  timing assumption starts driving the answer — measure that directly rather than proxying it.
- **Unconditional:** refuse when the denominator is at or below zero.

### D5 — The Plaid overlap (Q34)

Show **both** versions side by side — what was manually entered, what the institution sent — and let
the person choose. Remember the choice for **that transaction only**, never generalised to the
account.

Three requirements:
- A stable identity for the matched **pair** (account, date, type, amount, security), so the choice
  survives a re-sync.
- Ask **only where they genuinely differ**; take the feed silently when they agree, or the volume is
  unsurvivable.
- A rule for the **unmatched** — a hand-logged row with no counterpart, or vice versa: kept, flagged,
  never silently dropped.

Cap what is shown on a first sync (largest ten by amount).

### D6 — Affordability, replacing safe-to-spend (Q33)

- **Recurring cost:** estimated monthly expenses + new cost against income, stating the consequence —
  *"you would still cover it, with $350 a month less going to savings."* Carries an **end date** (a
  car payment stops), reusing the R8 mechanism.
- **One-off purchase:** impact on liquid assets and months of cover — *"$40,000 out of $95,000 takes
  you from 14 months to 8."*
- The plan-side question — does this change the odds — is **linked to**, not duplicated.

### D7 — Smaller builds

- **Property sale/purchase as two events** (Q9), with named sources of funds; improvements as
  one-time costs. No tax computed; flag when likely to matter. *Note: 1035 is annuities and life
  policies; the property provision is 1031, and since 2017 it does not cover a primary residence.*
- **Stepped part-time income** (Q45) — R8 mechanism, sign flipped. Link the step-down date to when
  employer health coverage ends. Note the Social Security earnings test interaction.
- **Pre-65 health premiums** (Q47) — same mechanism, with a growth rate **above** general inflation.
  Store health as a **spread**, not an absolute, so raising the general rate does not quietly make
  health cheaper.
- **Inflation lever** (Q50) — default to the 30-year average, stated with its window, fixed and dated
  rather than recomputed live; editable via "choices — try them."
- **Owner tag** (Q53) — you / partner / joint, **defaulted** to the primary person with one review
  screen. Joint accounts get a **third column at full value** (Q54), never a 50/50 split.
- **Survivor inputs** (Q4) — each spouse's benefit and claim age, each pension's survivor percentage
  and whether it inflates. **Optional.** Three states: complete, partial, empty — with the empty
  state naming exactly which fields would unlock it. Per R2, no field becomes a default.
- **Insurance labels** (Q11) — account page, under the name, small, in the institution's own wording.
  Scheme derived from institution type; **nothing shown when unknown**. SIPC needs its own clause: it
  covers broker failure, not investment losses. Never compute an uninsured amount.
- **Amber box** (Q15) — unclassified assets surface here, sorted **by money affected**, not by
  category. The donut switches from percentages to dollars when unclassified is large. Keep the words
  distinct: **unclassified** = a holding we could not sort; **unexplained** = the change-walk residual.
- **Expense-to-income ratio** (Q29) — TTM, both sides take-home. Above 100% is normal for a retiree
  drawing down: name what funded the gap.
- **Pacing** (Q35) — fixed bills by due date, flexible pro-rata, method stated on screen.
- **Debt-to-asset** (Q46) and **debt-payments-to-income** (Q13) — the latter's denominator is
  guaranteed income plus **planned** sustainable withdrawal, never actual withdrawals; employment
  income while working, switching at the retirement date. State the denominator.
