### Real vs nominal
Is this in today's money?

#### P1 — Display

| Condition | Case | Calculation or refusal |
|---|---|---|
| A missing | No live inflation figure (the feed failed or was never fetched) | Calculate anyway, at the stated fallback, and say so. The engine already fetches inflation and the 10-year yield separately with a per-figure fallback flag (master tab 2 row 4, ECONOMIC_FIGURES); the plan default is 2.5% when the store has nothing (src/domain/retirement/willItLast.ts:63). The screen line: "assuming 2.5% inflation — standard assumption, live figure unavailable." Never silently deflate at a rate the screen does not name. |
| B degraded | Headline inflation used for a retiree whose own costs rise faster (healthcare, insurance — spine row 57) | The headline rate is acceptable for display, named as the headline rate. Once 12+ months of the person's own categorised spending exist, offer their own basket rate as a labelled alternative. Never blend the two into one unnamed rate. |
| C shape-per-road | Inflation does not arrive per road; its three shapes are live series, stated fallback, and the person's own override | One rate at a time, and the figure records which of the three it used. Precedence reads one way: the person's override, once set, wins (same pattern as a confirmed tax type — a sync never undoes what the person said, master tab 2 row 6); otherwise the live figure; otherwise the stated fallback with its flag shown. |
| D time-bounded | A 3-day / 3-week / 3-month figure | Show nominal dollars with the actual dates; no deflation. At 2.5% a 3-month deflation moves a figure by about 0.6% — inside the noise, and the label would cost more attention than it buys. The basis label becomes mandatory from roughly 5 years out, where the gap is 13% and growing: $2,000,000 in 25 years at 2.5% inflation is about $1,080,000 in today's money ($2,000,000 ÷ 1.025²⁵ — the crosswalk's own trap cell rounds this to "$1.2m"; the division is shown so anyone can re-run it, and $1,080,000 is the number). Shown without the basis, that is not a number, it is a mood. |
| E related events | The person changes the inflation assumption, or the published series is revised | Recompute, and mark the change on any trend: "assumption changed on this date." A deflated series must never mix two rates across its own history — the step it creates would read as a market move. Keep the old assumption on the superseded figures (versioned, same rule as the snapshot correction rule, master tab 9 "Snapshot & honesty" row 12). |
| F account-type / confidence | A deflated figure's standing | A deflated figure is an estimate by construction: it carries "estimated — assumes X% inflation" and never the bare measured label. Deflation applies identically across account types; the tax adjustment is the next row's job. Never compound "in today's money" and "after tax" into one unlabelled number — apply each, and name both. |

### Pre-tax vs after-tax
Is this mine or partly the government's?

#### P1 — Balances

| Condition | Case | Calculation or refusal |
|---|---|---|
| A missing | Account tax type unknown (broker sent nothing, person has not answered) | Show the balance; estimate NO tax on it in either direction. Master K47 is the rule: when the tax type is unknown, tax must not be estimated at all — a guess here does not produce a slightly wrong figure, it produces a tax figure where the true answer may be zero. Screen: "How is this account taxed?" with the three-way answer; a confirmed answer is never overridden by a later sync (master tab 2 row 6, CONFIRM_TAX_TYPE). Addresses audit item 22's suppression half. |
| B degraded | Tax type is a guess from the broker's own words | The guess table marks a plain "IRA" as Traditional and CONFIDENT (master tab 2 row 12) — a Roth labelled simply "IRA" would show embedded tax it will never owe. Rule: an embedded-tax note appears only on a confirmed or unambiguously-worded account; a guessed account shows its guess and asks. The assumed future rate on deferred money is itself an assumption and is named on the note. |
| C shape-per-road | Connection sends broker words; a file carries no account type; manual asks the person | All three roads must end in the same three-way treatment — taxable, tax-deferred, tax-free — through the one rule (master tab 2 row 33, TAX_TREATMENT: explicit answer wins; PRE_TAX maps to deferred; ROTH to tax-free; default taxable, the cautious side). Whether the CSV import path asks for tax type today: (to verify). |
| D time-bounded | The age rules make deferred money time-locked, and this audience straddles the line | A deferred balance before 59½ generally carries a 10% penalty on top of the tax — with real exceptions in exactly this audience: leaving an employer at 55 or later unlocks that employer's 401(k), and substantially-equal periodic withdrawals unlock an IRA early. That is why the label says "not freely spendable before the age rules" (master K39 — a label that today lives only in that cell, audit item 18) and the app never computes a penalty dollar figure it cannot stand behind. A "what could I reach in 3 months" figure excludes or penalty-notes deferred money for anyone under 59½ — and at 55–70 both sides of that line are in the room. |
| E related events | Roth conversions, rollovers, RMDs | A conversion moves money from the deferred bucket to the tax-free bucket AND books the tax paid — it must never read as a withdrawal plus a contribution, which would corrupt every flow total. A 401(k)-to-IRA rollover is internal, not new money (the internal-transfer class, master tab 5 row 11). An RMD is a forced taxable event: money out of deferred, income on the tax side, whether or not it leaves the household. |
| F account-type / confidence | The after-tax view itself | After-tax net worth = net worth − (deferred balance × assumed income rate) − (unrealised gain in TAXABLE accounts × capital-gains rate). Inside an IRA or Roth there is no capital-gains framing, ever — those dollars are taxed as income on withdrawal or not at all. Worked line: a $1m Traditional IRA at an assumed 22% is about $780,000 of spendable money; shown equal to a $1m Roth it overstates the household by $220,000. Label: "roughly, if withdrawn today", assumptions listed — including that the surcharge on high incomes (3.8%) and state tax are or are not inside, which audit item 22 found unstated (tab 1 K54 says state tax is missing; tab 2 row 57 says the state rate is included — the two must be reconciled before the label can be written). |

| F account-type / confidence | Where the income lives decides whether it is taxable now | A dividend inside an IRA is not taxable income this year; the same dividend in a brokerage is. An income figure that adds the two and implies a tax bill is wrong on its face — split income totals by account tax treatment, and where the treatment is unknown, list the income but keep it out of every tax projection, with the ask from P1-A. |
