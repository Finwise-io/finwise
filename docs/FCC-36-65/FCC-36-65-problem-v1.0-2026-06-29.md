# FCC 36–65 — Problem / Gap / Need · v1.0 · 2026-06-29

**Step 1 of the gated process** (problem → strategy → high-level design → PRD → detailed design → UX).
Defines WHO this group is, WHAT'S broken for them, and WHAT'S MISSING today — *no solution yet.* Grounded in
the market-opportunity research (cradle-to-grave gap) + prior demographic research.

---

## 1. Who they are
**36–65 · ~128M people · 48% of US adults — the single largest and least-served band.** The "peak complexity"
years: the most financial decisions happening *at once*, the highest stakes, the most accounts, and the most
people depending on them (kids, aging parents). Income peaks here — but so do obligations.

It is **not one persona.** Three distinct sub-segments:

| Sub-segment | Life reality | Dominant money questions |
|---|---|---|
| **36–45 · Building** | First home (or trying), young kids, career growth, mortgage + student debt, starting to invest seriously, maybe first equity comp | "Can we afford the house / the second kid?" · "Am I saving enough while paying debt?" |
| **46–55 · Peak** | Peak earning *and* peak tax, kids → college, mortgage mid-life, equity comp/RSUs, retirement catch-up begins (50), aging parents emerging | "Is college going to wreck retirement?" · "How do I not overpay tax?" · "Am I on track?" |
| **56–65 · Pre-retirement** | The "am I going to be okay?" decade, sandwich caregiving peaks, retirement transition looms, Social Security / Medicare decisions approach, sequence-of-returns risk begins | "Can I actually retire, and when?" · "What do I do about Mom's finances and my kid's tuition at the same time?" |

## 2. The core problems (jobs-to-be-done), with evidence
1. **Fragmentation — "money in a dozen places, no single view."** Old 401(k)s, IRAs, brokerage, HSA, kids'
   529s, a spouse's accounts, home equity, multiple debts. No unified picture of *where I stand*.
2. **Multi-goal "am I on track?" anxiety.** Retirement, college, the house, debt payoff — *simultaneously*,
   with trade-offs between them. 54% of US households have **no dedicated retirement savings** (Fed SCF).
3. **Big-decision support — "can I afford X?"** The house, private school, a career change, a second kid,
   helping a parent. High-stakes, infrequent, and currently unsupported by any single tool.
4. **Sandwich-generation caregiving** — managing an aging parent's finances *and* a child's costs at once.
   This is the **single emptiest column** in the competitive research: **zero purpose-built apps.**
5. **Rising tax + comp complexity** — equity comp/RSUs, multiple income sources, peak bracket. "Partial"
   coverage at best today.
6. **The pre-retirement fog (56–65)** — genuinely not knowing "will I be okay," with the highest-stakes,
   least-reversible decisions (claiming, drawdown) approaching and no companion to walk them through it.

## 3. The market gap (what's unmet today)
From the competitive analysis (15 representative apps, 13 capabilities):
- **Apps are organized around tasks, not life transitions.** Budgeting apps are built for the young;
  retirement planners for the old. **Nobody owns the middle — or the transitions** between stages.
- **The emptiest, highest-stakes territories are unserved:** first home (0 purpose-built apps), kids/college
  (0), sandwich caregiving (0). Peak-earn/tax is "partial."
- **Broad trackers (Monarch, Empower, Simplifi) aggregate but don't guide** through decisions or transitions;
  **planners (Boldin, ProjectionLab) model the math but have weak daily money** and don't execute.
- **Result: people outgrow each single-purpose tool and abandon it** — finance-app day-30 retention is ~4%.
  Nothing is built to *follow them through* the peak-complexity decade into retirement.

## 4. The unmet need (the opportunity, at the problem level)
This group needs **one command center for the most complicated financial decade of their lives** that:
- gives a **unified view** across every account, person, and goal;
- answers **"am I on track?" across multiple simultaneous goals**, with the trade-offs visible;
- **supports the big decisions and life transitions** (afford-X, home, college, caregiving, the run-up to
  retirement) instead of stopping at a task;
- absorbs the **complexity** (multiple incomes, tax, equity comp, caregiving) **without overwhelming** them;
- gives a **recurring reason to open** (so it isn't a one-time calculator they abandon); and
- is **built to follow them into retirement** — so they never "outgrow" it (the FCC-65+ handoff).

## 5. Why this group is hard — and worth it
- **Hard:** high complexity, multiple stakeholders (spouse, kids, parents), a high trust bar (real money, real
  consequences), and decisions that are infrequent + irreversible.
- **Worth it:** highest willingness-to-pay sits exactly where competition is thinnest; they have real money and
  real anxiety, and they will pay for something that genuinely reduces both. It's also the on-ramp that, if we
  own the transition, retains them for *decades*.

## 6. What we still need to validate (open questions)
- Within 36–65, **which sub-segment + job is the sharpest entry** (the pre-retirement transition vs caregiving
  vs peak-tax)? — feeds the sequencing decision in Strategy (Step 2).
- The **daily/recurring hook** for this age (the "reason to open" that isn't young-person safe-to-spend).
- Real **willingness-to-pay** by job (the market research did not verify WTP — flagged).
