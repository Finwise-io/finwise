# Research: what 18–35-year-olds struggle with financially (2024–2026)

Deep-research run 2026-06-29 (5 search angles → 22 sources → 102 claims → 25 adversarially fact-checked,
21 confirmed / 4 killed). Used to pick the product wedge for the Employed-persona (18–35) launch.

## The verified picture (high confidence)
1. **Day-to-day cash flow is THE pain.** ~half live paycheck-to-paycheck (42% Gen Z BofA · 49% 18–35 Intuit ·
   >50% Deloitte · 56–59% other). Only **35% of Gen Z earn enough to save monthly**; a majority can't reliably
   save (YouGov). Over **a third can't cover a $400 emergency** in cash (Fed SHED).
2. **It's genuinely DAILY.** 22.5% of Gen Z worry about money *every day*, 56% at least weekly (YouGov). 16% of
   Americans feel money stress every single day; Gen Z has the **highest daily anxiety** of any generation
   (20%). 41% feel financial guilt weekly (BofA).
3. **Top stressors:** cost of living / inflation (69–76%), everyday expenses like groceries/utilities (61%),
   insufficient emergency savings (55–57%) — all **above** debt (43%), housing (37%), retirement (34%) (Bankrate).
4. **The coping behavior already exists:** when stressed, **69% check their bank balance** and **64% budget**
   (BofA). Budgeting is the #1 intervention young adults credit (41%, Intuit) — yet **24% have no budget**, only
   **9% feel "fully in control" of spending**, and **54% name impulse shopping** as their biggest challenge.
5. **#1 goal: build an emergency fund (32%)** — ahead of major purchases (25%) and debt repayment (21%) (YouGov).
6. **Visibility gap:** 35% spend more than they expected each month — the "where did my money go / can I afford
   this" problem an app can solve.

## Directional (lower confidence — appeared in fetch but not all fully verified)
- **Churn driver:** most budgeting apps are abandoned **within the first month** — a philosophy/behavior
  mismatch ("too much effort," esp. YNAB). Friction kills.
- **Pricing:** YNAB $14.99/mo ($109/yr, no free tier); Rocket Money free + $7–14/mo pay-what-you-want.
- Gen Z spends ~**$118/mo on subscriptions (+63% YoY)** — they DO pay, but are subscription-fatigued.
- Mint's 2023 shutdown orphaned millions (a gap nobody has fully captured).

## Honest gaps (NOT verified — flagged by the research)
- **Willingness-to-pay** specifics and **competitor churn** data did not survive verification — treat
  monetization + competitive positioning as *directional*, needs a focused follow-up before pricing.
- Deloitte headline figures are global; some "all US adults" stats aren't age-specific. Inflation-cycle
  dependent.

## Implication for the wedge
The data points hard at a **low-friction daily "where do I stand / can I afford this" hook** — the *answer*
budgeting produces, without the data-entry chore that gets apps abandoned. That, in turn, makes **low-friction
data (bank sync or near-zero manual input) a precondition**, not an optional V2 nicety.

Sources: Federal Reserve SHED 2024; YouGov 2025; Bankrate/YouGov; BofA Better Money Habits 2025/2026; Intuit
2025; Deloitte 2025 Gen Z & Millennial; Credit Karma 2024. Full claim/vote detail in the workflow output.
