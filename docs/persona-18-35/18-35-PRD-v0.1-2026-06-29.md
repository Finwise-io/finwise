# PRD — FinWise for Employed 18–35 (Safe-to-Spend wedge) · v0.1 · 2026-06-29

**Status:** DRAFT for approval (Gate 1 of: PRD → conceptual design → detailed design → build).
**Decisions locked:** Wedge = Safe-to-spend (+ Can-I-afford-X → emergency fund). Data = privacy-preserving
bank sync. Grounded in `18-35-research-v1.0-2026-06-29.md`.

---

## 1. Who (persona)
**Employed, 18–35, in the US — the "young earner trying to get stable."** Two sub-segments:
- **18–24** (student / early-career): low/irregular income, little net worth, retirement irrelevant, lives
  close to the edge, money stress is daily.
- **25–35** (establishing): real income but real obligations (rent, debt, maybe first investments), "where
  did my money go," starting to want a cushion and a plan.

What's true for both (from research): ~half live paycheck-to-paycheck, only ~35% can reliably save, a third
can't cover a $400 surprise, and money stress is **daily** — they already cope by **checking their balance**.

## 2. The wedge (the one sharp hook)
> **"Always know what's actually yours to spend right now — after bills, savings, and your goals are covered."**

Three surfaces of ONE engine, increasing depth:
1. **Safe-to-Spend (daily):** one number on Home — money available − upcoming bills before next payday −
   this period's savings/goal commitments. Updated automatically. *This is the daily habit.*
2. **Can I Afford X? (episodic + viral):** enter a purchase or new commitment → see the hit to your
   safe-to-spend, your cushion, and how long to recover. Shareable result.
3. **Starter emergency fund (the ladder):** their #1 stated goal. The engine suggests an auto-save toward a
   first $1,000 cushion and celebrates progress.

**Why this wedge (research-backed):** highest-frequency pain → daily habit → retention; it hooks an existing
behavior (69% already balance-check); it delivers the *answer* budgeting produces **without the data-entry
chore that gets apps abandoned in month one**.

## 3. Jobs to be done (what the user hires us for)
1. "Tell me what I can safely spend today without breaking my bills or savings." *(daily)*
2. "Can I afford this specific thing?" *(episodic)*
3. "Help me build a cushion so a surprise doesn't wreck me." *(goal)*
4. "Do it without me hand-entering every transaction." *(friction — the make-or-break)*
5. "Don't sell my data." *(trust — our differentiator)*

## 4. Data approach (the precondition)
**Privacy-preserving bank sync** — read-only aggregation of accounts/transactions, **stored + processed
on-device, encrypted, never sold or shared.** This is what makes a daily number possible without the chore.
- ⚠️ **Honest tension to resolve in design:** mainstream aggregators (e.g. Plaid) route data through their
  servers in transit — true 100%-on-device is hard. "Privacy-preserving" here = read-only, we store on-device,
  minimal retention, no sale/sharing, clear consent. **Needs a technical spike** (aggregator choice + the
  on-device storage model) before detailed design — flagged in §8.

## 5. Success metrics (north star + supporting)
- **North star: Day-30 retention** (does the daily habit stick?).
- **Activation:** % who connect an account AND see their first Safe-to-Spend number in session 1.
- **Engagement (leading):** weekly "number checks" per active user.
- **Value proof (justifies paying):** % who reach a $1,000 starter cushion.
- *(Monetization metric deferred — willingness-to-pay is unverified; see §8.)*

## 6. Scope — what's IN for v1 (and the RUTHLESS cut)
**IN (the Safe-to-Spend spine):**
- Onboarding → connect accounts (privacy-preserving sync) → first Safe-to-Spend number.
- Home: Safe-to-Spend hero + this-period view.
- Light auto-categorized spending visibility ("where it's going").
- Can I Afford X? simulator (shareable).
- Starter emergency-fund goal + auto-save nudges.
- Net worth (already built) — kept as the secondary "growth" view.
- Privacy/security UI (on-device, encrypted) + the privacy promise.

**OUT / deferred to V2 or the Retired mode (the hard cut — much of this is already built):**
- Retirement Monte-Carlo cockpit → Retired mode + 35+ (not this persona).
- Insurance, estate, tax-organizer, job-loss mode → V2.
- Deep investment performance/holdings tracking (Stocks/Bonds/Alternatives screens, Itemize) → keep net worth
  light; defer the depth.
- Heavy debt-payoff planner → keep debts visible in net worth; planner is a fast-follow.
- Couples / household.

> **This is the strategic call to confirm:** we de-emphasize a lot of *already-built* breadth to build the
> wedge (sync + the Safe-to-Spend experience) that **isn't built yet**. Quality of one loved thing > breadth.

## 7. Differentiation (why us)
- **The answer, not the chore** — vs the abandoned budgeting-app category.
- **Private by default** — vs Mint (dead) / data-monetizing incumbents.
- **Grows with you** — the wedge opens into the platform and, later, the Retired mode (you never switch apps).

## 8. Risks & open questions
- **Sync build + privacy tension** (§4) — the central technical decision; needs a spike.
- **Monetization unverified** — research could not confirm willingness-to-pay or competitor churn. Needs a
  focused follow-up before pricing; assume freemium + a $5–10/mo band as a placeholder only.
- **"Safe-to-spend" isn't novel** (Simple, Cleo, neobanks) — we win on execution + planning depth + privacy.
- **Cost-of-living salience is cycle-dependent** — but cash-flow anxiety is structural at this age.
